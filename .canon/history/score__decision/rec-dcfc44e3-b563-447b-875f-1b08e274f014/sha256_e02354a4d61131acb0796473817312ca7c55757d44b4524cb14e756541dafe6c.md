## Rationale

`execute-phase` tells the executor to run `git commit -m "<task-slug>"`, and the phase and store commits use the same one-line form. That is why recent subjects are bare slugs (`verdict-command`, `claims-manifest store`) with empty bodies. The subject stays the slug so `git log --oneline` still names the task or the phase. The body is one or two sentences on why the change was made.

## Counter-argument

A Conventional Commits subject (`feat`, `fix`, and a scope) is the usual way to read a log by kind of change. The slug is an internal id. A reader who was not in the session learns more from `fix(verdict): fail the claim when head pytest exits non-zero` than from a slug plus a body they have to open.
